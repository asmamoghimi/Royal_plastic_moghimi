Types of shrink film

It depends on your product and machine. Click a type to read more.

01

Packaging shrink

For fully wrapping or bundling products, from light retail boxes to group packs. Choose the thickness by product weight and the pressure of transport and storage.

02

Heat shrink

For L-sealers and heat guns; the film shrinks with heat and covers the product smoothly and neatly.

03

Bottle shrink

For 6 and 12-packs of water, drinks, detergents and oil. The heavier the pack, the thicker the film needs to be, usually 40 to 90 microns.

04

Tunnel shrink

For lines where the pack passes through a shrink tunnel. The film must match the tunnel temperature and line speed for even, flawless shrinkage.

05

Coloured

In the colour you choose, for branding or hiding the contents.

06

UV and anti-static

UV for packs that stay in the sun; anti-static to reduce dust and static.

Applications

Water and drinks

  • 6 and 12-packs of water, soft drinks and dairy drinks.
  • Suggested thickness: 40 to 60 microns.
  • Clear film so the bottle labels stay visible.
  • Matched to the shrink tunnel on your line.

Detergents, oil and heavy liquids

  • Dishwashing liquid, bleach and detergent bottles: about 50 to 70 microns.
  • Cooking oil and multi-litre containers: about 60 to 90 microns.
  • Prevents packs tearing during stacking and handling.
  • Strong film for heavy packs.

Food and retail

  • Chocolate, biscuit and light product boxes: thin film of about 20 to 25 microns.
  • Multipacks of cans and tins: about 25 to 40 microns.
  • A clean look on the store shelf.
  • Protection against dirt and moisture.

Cosmetics, personal care and pharma

  • Overwrapping boxes of cream, shampoo, soap and medicine.
  • Thin, highly transparent film (about 20 to 25 microns).
  • Clean shrinkage without wrinkles.
  • Promotional and gift packs.

Parts and tools

  • Packing spare parts and tools.
  • Suggested thickness: 30 to 50 microns for hard edges.
  • Resists tearing as it shrinks.
  • Protection in storage and transport.

Books, notebooks and printed goods

  • Wrapping books, notebooks and brochures.
  • Suggested thickness: 20 to 30 microns.
  • Heavier printed packs: thicker film.
  • Protection against moisture and scratches.

From granules to rolls

How shrink film is made at the Royal Plastic Moghimi factory.

01 Blending

Shrink-grade polyethylene granules are blended with the ordered colour or additives.

02 Blown extrusion

The film is blown into a bubble; this stretch is what later shrinks back with heat.

03 Cooling and thickness control

The film is cooled and its thickness controlled across the full width.

04 Slitting and forming

Cut as sheet, tube or half-tube at the ordered width.

05 Winding

The film is wound onto cores at the ordered roll weight.

06 Quality control and packing

Shrinkage, strength and dimensions are checked and the rolls are packed.

Ask an expert
What is shrink film?

A polyethylene film that shrinks when heated and wraps tightly around the product. In Iran it is also called “shirink” or “shiring” film.

Is thicker always better?

No. Film that is too thick raises cost for no reason, and film that is too thin tears. Choose the thickness by pack weight, product shape and transport conditions.

Can one film be used for all products?

Usually not. For example, water bottles and oil or detergent bottles need different thicknesses. If you have several products, tell us and we will recommend a film for each.

Does any film work in a shrink tunnel?

No. The film must match the tunnel temperature and your line speed to shrink evenly without holes or wrinkles. Tell us your machine when you order.

What is the difference between shrink and stretch film?

Stretch film is wrapped around the load under tension and needs no heat; shrink film tightens with heat and is better for multipacks and a clean retail look.

Do you make coloured or printed shrink film?

Yes. Clear film is standard and we also produce coloured versions. For logo printing, see our Printed Film page.
